STEP 1: HAZARD IDENTIFICATION

Lexical Hazards

* “menighetsarbeide” is not merely “church work” in a broad sense. Here it concerns the ordering, constitution, discipline, membership, and practical life of the local congregation. “Congregational work” best preserves that institutional and ecclesiological focus.
* “samfund” refers to organized church bodies or denominations, not merely “communities.” “Church bodies” is preferable.
* “Ledende principper” is the formal title of the Free Church’s governing ecclesiological principles. It should remain consistently “Leading Principles.”
* “verdslige folk” means spiritually “worldly people,” not simply secular persons in the modern sociological sense.
* “bekjender sig for troende” means “profess themselves to be believers,” with an important distinction between profession and actual faith.
* “naademidlerne” is the doctrinally fixed Lutheran term “the means of grace.”
* “naadegaverne” should remain distinct from “means of grace”; “gifts of grace” preserves the terminology without collapsing the two concepts.
* “vækkende forkyndelse” is revival/awakening preaching in the older Lutheran-pietistic sense: preaching directed toward awakening the spiritually indifferent or unconverted. “Awakening preaching” retains this theological force.
* “gjenstridige syndere” means obstinate or persistently resistant sinners, not merely troublesome sinners.
* “massemenighet” is a polemical ecclesiological term for a congregation embracing a religiously mixed mass rather than a consciously believing membership. “Mass congregation” is somewhat foreign in English but preserves the historical terminology better than explanatory paraphrase.
* “seile under falsk flag” is the idiom “sail under false colors,” implying falsely presenting one’s ecclesiastical identity.
* “stemplet for hyklere” is strong: “branded as hypocrites” or “stamped as hypocrites.” “Branded” preserves the accusatory force.
* “tilknytningspunkt” means a “point of contact” or point at which spiritual engagement becomes possible.
* “te sig … som from” means to present or display oneself before others as pious.
* “nidkjær” is “zealous,” with biblical and devotional resonance.
* “opdra folket” here means to train, form, or educate the congregation into adherence to certain ecclesiological principles, not child-rearing.
* “hulheten” means the hollowness or internal inconsistency of the procedure.
* “samfulde tre aar” emphasizes the entire duration: “a full three years.”

Syntactic Hazards

* The article repeatedly builds its argument through long sentences in which accusation, qualification, and conclusion accumulate. These should not be fragmented merely for modern readability.
* Anderson relies heavily on rhetorical questions. They are integral to the polemic and must remain questions rather than being converted into declarative explanation.
* Several paragraphs turn on contrasts introduced by “Men,” “Ja, men,” “Ti,” and similar conjunctions. Their argumentative function must remain explicit.
* The quoted portions of the Leading Principles have a formal, didactic syntax. They should retain their internal logical ordering.
* Parenthetical remarks carry argumentative weight and should not be reduced to incidental asides.
* The repeated sequence “either … or …” near the center of the article is structurally important because it forces the reader toward a practical ecclesiastical choice.
* The final section moves from a hypothetical objection into personal confession and then into a conclusion about consistency. That sequence must remain visible.

Theological Hazards

* The distinction between the visible or “external” organization of the congregation and the congregation understood spiritually as consisting of true believers is central.
* “believing,” “non-believing,” “hypocrite,” and “worldly” are not interchangeable categories in Anderson’s argument.
* The means of grace and gifts of grace must remain distinct.
* Church discipline is presented as a “holy calling” and includes awakening preaching, admonition, reminder, and exclusion of openly obstinate sinners.
* Anderson disputes not church discipline itself but the inference that an unbelieving person seeking formal membership should therefore be designated a hypocrite.
* “truth” functions as a spiritual and theological category, not merely factual correctness.
* The work of “the Word and the Holy Spirit” must retain explicitly Lutheran agency: persons become believing through divine action rather than merely religious education.
* “the Lord’s cause” has devotional and biblical resonance and should not become merely “church activity.”

Voice Hazards

* Anderson writes respectfully but sharply toward Professor Harbo, whom he calls his “esteemed teacher.” The coexistence of deference and serious criticism must remain.
* Statements such as “it would be entirely wasted time” should not be softened into diplomatic modern academic prose.
* His self-criticism is severe: he accuses himself and others of deliberately transgressing principles they profess to uphold.
* The image of removing a foundation stone must retain its destabilizing force.
* “sailing under false colors” deliberately introduces the possibility of ecclesiastical dishonesty and should remain morally pointed.
* The final confession that it took him three full years to recognize the hollowness of his procedure should retain its tone of regret and self-indictment.

STEP 2: IDIOM ANALYSIS

* “seile under falsk flag” literally means to sail under a false flag. The natural older English idiom is “sail under false colors.” It implies representing oneself as belonging to a cause or identity one does not actually practice.
* “at ripe ut en av grundstenene i en bygning” means tearing or wrenching out one of a building’s foundation stones. The image is ecclesiological: removing a foundational principle makes the entire structure unstable.
* “stik motsat av de linjer hvorefter menigheten var organiseret” means directly contrary to the principles or lines along which the congregation had been organized. “Lines” can be preserved because the metaphor concerns a governing pattern.
* “lar sløret glide tilside” means “let the veil slip aside,” revealing one’s real condition. The metaphor should remain intact rather than becoming simply “reveal their true motives.”
* “viser sig i sin sande skikkelse” means appearing in one’s true form or character.
* “tilknytningspunkt mellem den troende og den ikke-troende sjæl” is pietistic-pastoral language. The longing for truth is the “point of contact” between believer and unbelieving soul; Anderson means that such longing provides the basis for spiritual approach and pastoral engagement.
* “Herrens sak” corresponds naturally to older devotional English “the Lord’s cause.”
* “vækkende forkyndelse” belongs to the language of awakening movements and pietism. A literal but historically meaningful “awakening preaching” is preferable to “evangelistic preaching,” which would import later English-language categories.
* “aabenbare og gjenstridige syndere” has biblical-church-discipline resonance. “Open and obstinate sinners” preserves both public manifestation and persistent refusal.
* “massemenighet” is a historically loaded Free Church criticism of congregations constituted without sufficient regard to regenerate or confessing membership. A literal rendering is preferable because explanatory substitution would erase the polemical vocabulary.
* Pontoppidan refers to the Lutheran catechetical tradition associated with Erik Pontoppidan. Anderson’s point is deliberately provocative: something may be supported by Pontoppidan and yet still require the question, “But is it biblical?”

STEP 3: ARGUMENT STRUCTURE IDENTIFICATION

* Opening thesis: Anderson responds to Professor Harbo’s request for a more precise explanation of the difference between the Free Church conception of congregational work and that of the larger church bodies.
* First rebuttal: he argues that such an explanation ought not be necessary because the matter has been repeatedly discussed throughout the Free Church’s existence.
* Central assertion: a substantial difference must exist; otherwise the Free Church congregations lacked justification for refusing the previous year’s union.
* First major pivot: Anderson distinguishes between what the Leading Principles teach and what the Lutheran Free Church actually practices.
* Self-implication: he includes himself among those who do not carry the Leading Principles consistently into practice.
* Concrete contradiction: congregations are organized from “worldly people,” and persons not professing faith are admitted.
* Doctrinal comparison: point 2 defines the congregation as consisting of believers; point 4 recognizes unbelieving members of its external organization and calls such persons hypocrites.
* Rhetorical challenge: Anderson asks whether deliberately admitting unbelievers can genuinely be harmonized with these principles.
* Historical charge: he recalls earlier Free Church warnings against the “mass congregation.”
* Ethical conclusion: those whose practice contradicts the principles must either return to consistent observance or leave the Free Church.
* Personal resolution: Anderson has chosen the latter.
* Second major thesis: he no longer believes the Leading Principles should be carried through in congregational work.
* Specific doctrinal objection: he rejects the designation “hypocrite” for an unbelieving person merely because that person seeks admission to the congregation’s external organization.
* Qualification: he affirms the latter portion of point 4 concerning preaching, admonition, and exclusion of open and obstinate sinners.
* Biblical challenge: he distinguishes between unbelievers seeking association with the congregation and true hypocrites who resist truth while presenting themselves as pious.
* Experiential corroboration: in his pastoral experience, genuinely obstinate sinners avoid congregations characterized by awakening preaching and earnest admonition.
* Positive evidence: some who appeared thoroughly worldly when joining later became earnest believers through the Word and Holy Spirit.
* Anticipated objection: has he taught his congregation the Free Church principles?
* Response: his congregation had never formally adopted those principles and had originally been organized along contrary lines.
* Practical consequence: attempting to impose the principles would have produced upheaval harmful to the congregation.
* Final self-judgment: he regrets that it took three years to recognize the hollowness of this inconsistent arrangement.
* Final principle: consistency in congregational work is necessary if the Lord’s blessing is to be expected.

STEP 4: BIBLICAL ECHO IDENTIFICATION

* “the congregation consists of believing persons” reflects New Testament understandings of the church as the assembly of saints or believers, though the statement itself comes from the Free Church’s Leading Principles.
* “means of grace” is confessional Lutheran terminology grounded especially in the ministry of Word and Sacrament rather than a direct biblical quotation.
* “gifts of grace” evokes New Testament χάρισμα language, especially Romans 12 and 1 Corinthians 12.
* “open and obstinate sinners” and exclusion from the congregation echo Matthew 18:15–18 and 1 Corinthians 5.
* “hypocrites” strongly evokes Jesus’ denunciations of hypocrisy, especially Matthew 6 and Matthew 23.
* Anderson’s description of persons who “hardened themselves against the truth” echoes biblical hardening language found throughout the Gospels, Acts, and Romans.
* His description of people who wished to appear pious before others resembles Matthew 6:1–18 and Matthew 23.
* “through the working of the Word and the Holy Spirit” reflects Lutheran biblical theology regarding the Spirit working through the Word; likely scriptural resonances include Romans 10:17 and passages concerning the Spirit’s work through proclamation.
* “zealous for the Lord’s cause” recalls biblical exhortations to zeal, including Romans 12:11, though no direct quotation is evident.
* “foundation stones” belongs broadly to biblical church-building imagery, including 1 Corinthians 3, Ephesians 2, and 1 Peter 2, though Anderson does not appear to quote any one passage directly.

STEP 5: VOICE INTENSITY PRESERVATION

* Anderson’s charge of inconsistency must remain explicit rather than being reframed as a mere institutional tension.
* “deliberately transgress” preserves the moral seriousness of knowingly acting against professed principles.
* The choice between returning to the principles and leaving the Free Church must remain categorical.
* His rejection of the designation “hypocrite” must retain its sharp interrogative form: “But is it biblical?”
* His criticism of himself must remain equally strong; the article is not merely an attack upon others.
* The concluding appeal to divine blessing depends upon practical consistency and must retain that consequential force.

STEP 6: MODAL FORCE PRESERVATION

* “burde ikke være paakrævet” = “ought not to be necessary,” not merely “need not perhaps be necessary.”
* “maa jo ha” = “must, of course, have,” retaining necessity.
* “skal kunne faa … til at gaa” = “if we are to make … function,” preserving the condition of necessity.
* “bør jo være” = “ought surely to be,” not “ideally would be.”
* “maa være konsekvent” = “must be consistent.”
* “skal kunne vente” = “if we are to expect,” retaining a necessary condition rather than a suggestion.
* “kan umulig faa dem med” = “it is impossible to get them to join,” preserving impossibility.

STEP 8: METAPHOR INTEGRITY PRESERVATION

* The foundation-stone image will remain a literal building metaphor.
* “sail under false colors” will retain the nautical metaphor.
* “the veil slip aside” will remain rather than being flattened into psychological explanation.
* “true form” will retain the visible-disclosure imagery.
* “lines along which the congregation was organized” will preserve the organizing-line metaphor.

STEP 9: TONE CONTRAST PRESERVATION

* The opening impatience toward Harbo contrasts with Anderson’s respectful reference to him as his teacher.
* The institutional critique becomes personal self-accusation when Anderson admits that he himself has not practiced the principles consistently.
* Severe criticism of point 4 is immediately balanced by affirmation that its teaching concerning preaching, admonition, and church discipline is biblical.
* The warning concerning hypocrites is contrasted with pastoral openness toward unbelievers who may possess an inward longing for truth.
* The description of obstinate sinners is severe; the account of formerly worldly members becoming believers is hopeful.
* The conclusion returns from pastoral hope to sober self-judgment and the necessity of consistency.

STEP 10: ORAL CADENCE AWARENESS

* Long interrogative sequences will be retained, particularly where Anderson presses the reader from premise to consequence.
* Repetitions such as “And has there not … ? And has there not … ?” will remain because they reproduce spoken polemical pressure.
* Semicolons and dashes will be used where the Norwegian accumulates rather than resolves thought.
* Short questions such as “But is it biblical?” will remain isolated because their abruptness supplies rhetorical force.
* The progression from quotation to question, question to accusation, and accusation to personal decision will not be smoothed into academic exposition.

STEP 11: ACADEMIC TRANSLATION

ANSWER

“Folkebladet,” August 7, 1918

(L. O. Anderson.)
——

When, in Folkebladet for July 17, I am requested by Prof. Harbo to give a more precise explanation of wherein consists the difference between the view of congregational work which the Lutheran Free Church maintains through the Leading Principles and the view of congregational work which the large church bodies maintain, I will say, first of all, that it ought not to be necessary for me to have to give Prof. Harbo a more detailed explanation of a matter which, more than almost any other matter, has been discussed and explained again and again by men within the Lutheran Free Church, in speech and in writing, ever since the Lutheran Free Church began its existence as an independent church body. It would be altogether wasted time for me to attempt it. In my previous article I pointed out the difference, and I will not enter further into it on the present occasion.

That there is a difference—a considerable difference—between the view of congregational work maintained in the Leading Principles and that maintained in the church bodies’ rules for congregational work is something that Free Church people, least of all, will dispute. If there is no difference—and that an essential difference—then the Free Church congregations have no valid excuse for having refrained from joining the union last year.

---

Upon looking more closely, one will find that in my previous article I do not make the expression, the view of congregational work maintained in the Leading Principles, identical with the expression, the view of congregational work maintained in the Lutheran Free Church, as my esteemed teacher does in his article. To make these two expressions identical would not come sufficiently near the truth. For a large part of those who profess adherence to the Free Church—I among them—do not carry into effect in congregational work what is maintained on this point in the Leading Principles. We both organize congregations out of worldly people and admit into the congregation persons who do not profess themselves to be believers. Is this right according to the Leading Principles?

But, it is said, we must have an external organization if we are to make congregational work function, and this is in harmony with the Leading Principles, point 3. Yes, but point 2 emphasizes that “the congregation consists of believing persons who, through the use of the means of grace and the gifts of grace according to the direction of God’s Word, seek their own salvation and eternal blessedness and that of all their fellow human beings.” Someone will immediately remark that by this is meant the congregation in the spiritual sense, the true believers, whom alone the Lord acknowledges as belonging to the congregation of God. Then go to point 4. And let us take it as it stands. Principles intended to show us the way in congregational work ought surely to be so readily understandable that we can understand what is meant without having to use a commentary. In point 4 it says:

“Since not all members of the congregation’s external organization are always believing persons, and since such hypocrites often take false comfort from their connection with the congregation, it is the congregation’s holy calling to cleanse itself both through awakening preaching of God’s Word, through earnest admonition and reminder, and through the exclusion of open and obstinate sinners.”

Does it follow from point 4 that we have the right to establish a congregation out of worldly people? Does it appear to be in harmony with the Leading Principles that we deliberately admit non-believing persons—that is, “hypocrites”—into the congregation? Have not the church bodies been referred to as those who conduct congregational work in this manner? And has there not, especially in the Free Church’s earliest period, been earnest warning against this, which has been called the “mass congregation”?

What, then, are we to do? Do we act rightly toward the Free Church and toward ourselves by continuing to remain connected with the Free Church, while at the same time continuing deliberately to transgress the Leading Principles in so important a field as the work of the local congregation? To approve this—would it not be like wrenching out one of the foundation stones of a building?—an act that would render the whole building insecure. Is it not time that we who do not carry the Leading Principles into practice either turn back and begin to practice what we have professed—so that we may no longer have to expose ourselves to the charge of sailing under false colors—or else leave the Free Church and go where, according to our practice, we belong?

---

I have chosen the latter. In saying this I also say, of course, that I do not believe in carrying the Leading Principles through in congregational work. From what has been stated above, it is not difficult to guess the reason.

In the Leading Principles, point 4, those persons who enter the congregation’s external organization and are not believers are branded as hypocrites. (Would not point 4 have been much better in the form in which it originally stood, as it came from Sverdrup’s pen? See Guidance in the Principles of the Lutheran Free Church, page 50.) I say nothing against the latter part of point 4, that it is “the congregation’s holy calling to cleanse itself both through awakening preaching of God’s Word, through earnest admonition and reminder, and through the exclusion of open and obstinate sinners.” That is biblical. But is it also biblical to brand persons as hypocrites when they join the congregation’s external organization and are not believing persons? It is, according to Pontoppidan. But is it biblical?

I have found nothing in the Bible that urges me to brand as a hypocrite the person who, without being a believer, requests admission into the congregation’s external organization. But I have found passages in the Bible that encourage me rather to regard the desire of non-believing persons to join the congregation’s external organization as an expression of an inward longing for the truth. And precisely the longing for the truth is the point of contact between the believing and the non-believing soul, and ought to make us cautious, so that neither in speech nor in writing, neither in doctrinal statement nor in principles for congregational work, do we give it to be understood that such a person is a hypocrite. The Savior never gave any such thing to be understood when he dealt with non-believing persons. Nor did the apostles! It was persons who outright hardened themselves against the truth and who nevertheless wished to present themselves before others as pious whom the Lord and the apostles called hypocrites. (That hypocrites are found in the congregation among those who profess themselves to be believers is granted, and shall not be discussed further here.)

My experience in congregational work confirms what has been stated above. Where there is “awakening preaching of God’s Word, earnest admonition and reminder,” there “obstinate sinners” will not enter the congregation. It is impossible to get them to join, whether one attempts to entice them or to use force. They will sooner let the veil slip aside and show themselves in their true form. Furthermore, several who gave the impression of being thoroughly worldly when they enrolled in the congregation have since, through the working of the Word and the Holy Spirit, become earnest believers, zealous for the Lord’s cause.

---

But have you laid the Free Church’s principles before your congregation, and have you attempted to train the people so that they might be able to acknowledge the principles and carry them into practice?

The fact is that our congregation is one of the many congregations that have professed adherence to the Free Church but have not, by congregational resolution, acknowledged the Free Church’s principles and rules and reported this to the secretary of the organization committee (see Revised Rules, point 4), and which consequently, strictly speaking, have not belonged to the Free Church. But how could I have done so? It would have been meaningless to attempt to train the people of the congregation to acknowledge and carry into practice something directly contrary to the lines along which the congregation had been organized. The congregation had, after all, been established before I came, without the slightest regard for the Leading Principles. They were not even read aloud. Had we attempted to secure acknowledgment of the principles, it would certainly have caused an upheaval, to the injury of the congregational work here.

I regret that it took me a full three years to come to a clear understanding of the hollowness of this procedure and to learn to act consistently. We must be consistent in congregational work if we are to expect the Lord to bless it.

The translation retains “mass congregation,” “awakening preaching,” “external organization,” and “Leading Principles” as historically and theologically marked terminology rather than replacing them with smoother modern ecclesiastical equivalents.
