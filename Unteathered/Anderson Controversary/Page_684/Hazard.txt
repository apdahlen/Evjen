STEP 1: HAZARD IDENTIFICATION

Lexical Hazards

* “Past.” is an abbreviation for “Pastor.” In the title and body it functions as an ecclesiastical title, not as an adjective.
* The title gives “Past. O. L. Anderson,” while the article itself repeatedly gives “Past. L. O. Anderson.” This appears to be a typographical or editorial inconsistency in the source and should not be silently corrected in the title.
* “gjort rede for” means “given an account of” or “explained,” with a somewhat formal argumentative sense.
* “Kort og godt” is idiomatic: “In short” or “Briefly stated,” rather than literally “short and good.”
* “blit klar over” means “come to realize,” “become convinced,” or “come to see clearly.”
* “menighetsarbeide” is literally “congregational work,” but in this ecclesial context it means the work carried on in and for the local congregation. “Church work” is broader and could blur the local-congregational emphasis.
* “Ledende principper” is the formal title of a document or body of principles. It should be rendered consistently as “Guiding Principles.”
* “de store samfund” refers to the larger church bodies or denominations. “Societies” would be misleading in English.
* “hævder” here means “maintains,” “holds,” or “upholds,” not “claims” in a merely contentious sense.
* “staa i Den lutherske frikirke” means to remain within, belong to, or hold one’s position in the Lutheran Free Church.
* “praktisere … efter det syn” means to conduct one’s ministry in accordance with that view.
* “erkjendelse” carries the sense of recognition or realization reached through reflection.
* “ti” is the older spelling of “thi,” meaning “for,” “because.”
* “drives,” of congregational work, means “be carried on” or “conducted.”
* “samfundene” again means the church bodies/denominations, not voluntary associations in a generic sense.
* “liv og trivsel” is a paired expression meaning “life and flourishing/well-being.” “Prosperity” would be too secular.
* “efter sit væsen” means “according to its essential nature,” not merely according to its outward character.
* “kald og opgave” means “calling and task,” with “calling” retaining theological weight.
* “hvori … bestaar” means “wherein … consists.”
* “rette en venlig anmodning” is a formal idiom: “address a friendly request” or “make a friendly request.”
* “fremstaa” is repeated rhetorically and should remain repeated: “appear,” “come forth,” or “stand forth.”
* “levende og fri menighet” is ecclesiologically charged: a “living and free congregation,” not simply a lively and independent congregation.
* “nytestamentlige skikkelse” means its “New Testament form” or “New Testament shape,” referring to the church as it appears in the apostolic witness.
* “aandsfyldt” means “Spirit-filled,” with a clear Christian theological sense.
* “daad­rig” means active, vigorous in deeds, energetic in action. “Rich in deeds” is possible but less natural.
* “født av Gud” is biblical language: “born of God.”
* “omfatter med varm interesse” means “regard with warm interest” or “take a warm interest in.”
* “yde vor skjærv” is idiomatic and biblical-cultural: literally to contribute one’s small portion, comparable to contributing one’s “mite.”
* “kastes … lys derover” means “light may be shed upon it.”
* “Guds rike og sjæles frelse” means “the kingdom of God and the salvation of souls,” a conventional theological pairing with considerable spiritual force.
* “overmaate” means “exceedingly,” “extremely,” or “of the greatest,” and should retain its emphatic force.

Syntactic Hazards

* The prose relies on long cumulative sentences in which a premise is stated, restated, and then pressed toward a conclusion. Breaking these into short modern sentences would weaken the polemical and sermonic logic.
* The paragraph beginning “Det første som vi lægger merke til …” accumulates several subordinate clauses before arriving at the practical consequence of Anderson’s distinction. The repetition is deliberate.
* Harbo repeatedly restates Anderson’s claim in slightly varied language. This is argumentative emphasis, not redundancy to be edited away.
* “idet” introduces an explanatory clause: the two views are different “in that” the larger church bodies hold one view and the Free Church another.
* “Og saa helt forskjellige …” intensifies the previous assertion and moves from conceptual difference to institutional incompatibility.
* “nemlig” functions as an explicative marker and should remain logically explicit.
* “selvfølgelig” appears repeatedly as part of the author’s argumentative pressure: what follows is presented as an unavoidable inference.
* “ti det er jo ret at slutte …” combines causal explanation with an appeal to obvious reason: “for it is surely right to conclude…”
* The sentence beginning “Og det er dette syn …” contains a purpose clause embedded within the definition of the disputed ecclesial view. Its cumulative form should be preserved.
* The sequence “fremstaa … fremstaa … fremstaa …” is anaphoric and should not be collapsed.
* The final long sentence builds from the question itself, to those concerned with it, to their willingness to contribute, to the purpose of illumination, to greater clarity, and finally to the stakes: God’s kingdom and souls’ salvation. Its architecture is central to the rhetoric.

Theological Hazards

* “menigheten” is not simply an organization but the Christian congregation understood theologically.
* The author’s argument assumes that one’s practice of congregational ministry arises from one’s doctrine of the congregation—what it is “according to its essence,” and what its divine “calling and task” are.
* “levende og fri menighet” likely reflects Free Church ecclesiology: spiritual life and evangelical freedom are not merely sociological characteristics.
* “nytestamentlige skikkelse” carries a normative claim that the congregation ought to correspond to the New Testament pattern.
* “aandsfyldt” must be rendered “Spirit-filled,” not merely “spiritual” or “inspired.”
* “født av Gud” is Johannine regeneration language and should remain explicitly biblical.
* “Guds rike og sjæles frelse” must retain both the corporate kingdom dimension and the individual soteriological dimension.
* “frugt til menighetens liv og trivsel” evokes biblical fruit imagery and should retain “bear fruit” rather than being flattened to “produce beneficial results.”

Voice Hazards

* Harbo’s apparent gratitude to Anderson is rhetorically pointed: Anderson has, perhaps unwittingly, admitted that a real ecclesiological difference exists between the Free Church and the larger denominations.
* The repetition of “Pastor Anderson” and “Den lutherske frikirke” contributes to the formal, public, polemical character of the article.
* The tone is firm but not abusive. “venlig anmodning” marks a deliberate courteous restraint within controversy.
* Phrases such as “selvfølgelig,” “uten noget forbehold,” and “saa overmaate betydningsfuldt” should not be weakened, because they sustain the author’s confidence and urgency.
* The final paragraph shifts from controversy toward common spiritual responsibility. That tonal widening must remain evident.
* The author does not present the ecclesiological question as an abstract institutional matter; he connects it directly with the kingdom of God and the salvation of souls.

STEP 2: IDIOM ANALYSIS

* “Kort og godt”

  * Meaning: “In short,” “to put it briefly and plainly.”
  * A literal rendering such as “short and good” would be meaningless in English.
  * Best equivalent in the quotation: “In short.”

* “blit klar over”

  * Meaning: to have reached a clear realization.
  * “I have come to realize” preserves the reflective development implied by the phrase.

* “staa i Den lutherske frikirke”

  * Meaning: remain within or continue as part of the Lutheran Free Church.
  * Literal “stand in” would obscure the institutional sense.

* “praktisere … efter det syn”

  * Meaning: to conduct congregational ministry according to a particular ecclesiological understanding.
  * “conduct my congregational work according to” retains both practice and principle.

* “gjort den opdagelse eller kommet til den erkjendelse”

  * The double expression deliberately stresses that Anderson has now recognized a distinction the author regards as important.
  * Translation should preserve both halves rather than compress them to “realized.”

* “det er jo ret at slutte”

  * Meaning: it is surely/properly reasonable to infer.
  * “for it is surely right to conclude” retains the author’s didactic logic.

* “liv og trivsel”

  * An established paired phrase.
  * Here it means spiritual life and healthy flourishing of the congregation.
  * “life and flourishing” is preferable to the more secular “life and prosperity.”

* “rette en venlig anmodning”

  * Formal public-discourse idiom.
  * “address a friendly request to Pastor Anderson” preserves period formality.

* “fremstaa”

  * The repeated verb suggests becoming manifest in its true form.
  * “stand forth” is elevated but can sound artificially biblical; “appear among us” better preserves the didactic rather than KJV-like tone.
  * Because the original repetition is important, the English should repeat “appear.”

* “født av Gud”

  * Direct biblical expression, especially associated with 1 John and the Johannine writings.
  * Natural English biblical equivalent: “born of God.”
  * No archaic pronouns are necessary.

* “yde vor skjærv”

  * Literally to contribute one’s small share.
  * It may carry an echo of the widow’s mite, though the phrase had also become a general idiom.
  * “contribute our share” preserves the meaning without forcing an allusion that may be only implicit.

* “kastes … lys derover”

  * Conventional metaphor: “that all possible light may be shed upon it.”
  * The metaphor should remain intact.

STEP 3: ARGUMENT STRUCTURE IDENTIFICATION

* Opening factual premise:

  * Anderson has publicly explained why he left the Lutheran Free Church.

* Anderson’s stated reason:

  * His manner of conducting congregational work does not accord with the Lutheran Free Church’s Guiding Principles.
  * His practice is instead more harmonious with the view of the larger denominations.

* Harbo’s first inference:

  * Anderson’s statement necessarily implies that two substantially different understandings of congregational work exist.
  * The difference is sufficiently serious that Anderson could not remain in the Lutheran Free Church while working according to the other understanding.

* Polemical consequence:

  * Harbo welcomes Anderson’s acknowledgment because it contradicts those who claim that no real difference exists between the Lutheran Free Church and the denominations.

* Transition from practice to doctrine:

  * The question is not merely methodological.
  * A person’s view of congregational work depends on his prior view of what the congregation essentially is and what its calling and task are.

* Identified omission in Anderson’s explanation:

  * Anderson does not specify wherein the two views differ.
  * Nor does he identify which provisions of the Guiding Principles express the Free Church position.

* Public request:

  * Harbo therefore asks Anderson to explain both matters in greater detail.

* Broader pastoral/ecclesiological appeal:

  * The issue concerns how the congregation may become manifest as living, free, New Testament-shaped, Spirit-filled, and active.
  * All who are born of God should therefore care about the issue and contribute to its clarification.

* Concluding stakes:

  * Greater clarity is needed because the matter concerns the kingdom of God and the salvation of souls.
  * The author then deliberately suspends further discussion: “But this time we shall say no more about this question.”

STEP 4: BIBLICAL ECHO IDENTIFICATION

* “bære frugt” — “bear fruit”

  * Broad New Testament resonance, especially John 15:5, 8, 16; Matthew 7:17–20; Galatians 5:22.
  * Here it refers to congregational work producing genuine spiritual life and flourishing.

* “levende … menighet” — “living congregation”

  * Not necessarily a direct quotation, but consonant with New Testament imagery of the church as a living body, especially 1 Corinthians 12 and Ephesians 4.

* “nytestamentlige skikkelse”

  * Not itself a quotation, but explicitly invokes the ecclesial pattern witnessed in the New Testament, especially Acts and the Pauline church texts.

* “aandsfyldt” — “Spirit-filled”

  * Resonates strongly with Acts 2:4; Ephesians 5:18 and the broader Lukan description of Spirit-filled believers and communities.

* “født av Gud” — “born of God”

  * Strong Johannine echo.
  * Likely sources include John 1:13; 1 John 3:9; 4:7; 5:1, 4, 18.

* “Guds rike”

  * Central biblical expression throughout the Synoptic Gospels and the New Testament.

* “sjæles frelse” — “salvation of souls”

  * Possible resonance with 1 Peter 1:9, “the salvation of your souls,” though the expression is also standard devotional terminology.

STEP 5: VOICE INTENSITY PRESERVATION

* Anderson’s statement must be allowed to bear the full argumentative weight Harbo places upon it.
* Harbo’s repeated insistence that the two views are “entirely different” should not be diluted to “somewhat different.”
* “uten noget forbehold” requires “without any qualification/reservation.”
* The movement from practical church work to the essential nature of the congregation must remain categorical: practice “depends” upon ecclesiology.
* The importance of the matter must remain emphatic because Harbo explicitly ties it to the kingdom of God and salvation of souls.
* The courtesy of the request must coexist with the pressure of the argument; the translation should neither make Harbo combative nor make him mild.

STEP 6: MODAL FORCE PRESERVATION

* “kunde ikke staa” — “could not remain” retains impossibility/incompatibility.
* “bør drives” — “ought to be carried on” preserves normative obligation; “is carried on” would weaken it.
* “skal kunne arbeide” — in context, “how we may be able to work” or “how we are to work.” The latter better preserves the deliberative and normative force.
* “kan bære frugt” — “may bear fruit” retains possibility contingent upon right practice.
* “skal vi ikke sige mer” — “we shall say no more” retains deliberate resolve.

STEP 7: CONSISTENT THEOLOGICAL TERM RENDERING

* menighet = congregation
* menighetsarbeide / menighetsarbeidet = congregational work
* syn = view
* Ledende principper = Guiding Principles
* samfund / samfundene = church bodies / denominations, according to syntax; “larger church bodies” is preferred where “de store samfund” occurs
* væsen = essential nature
* kald = calling
* opgave = task
* bære frugt = bear fruit
* liv = life
* trivsel = flourishing
* født av Gud = born of God
* Guds rike = kingdom of God
* sjæles frelse = salvation of souls
* aandsfyldt = Spirit-filled

STEP 8: METAPHOR INTEGRITY PRESERVATION

* “bære frugt” remains “bear fruit.”
* “kastes … lys” remains “light may be shed.”
* “fremstaa” retains the image of the congregation becoming manifest or appearing in its proper form rather than being paraphrased as “function successfully.”
* “levende” remains “living,” preserving organic and spiritual imagery.
* “yde vor skjærv” may be rendered “contribute our share”; the image of a small personal contribution is thereby retained without introducing an unnecessarily archaic English idiom.

STEP 9: TONE CONTRAST PRESERVATION

* The article begins as factual report.
* It then becomes pointedly analytical and mildly polemical.
* Harbo’s “gratitude” introduces restrained irony or at least argumentative satisfaction.
* The article then becomes doctrinal, moving beneath methods to ecclesiology.
* The request to Anderson is explicitly courteous.
* The final paragraph expands into earnest pastoral concern, with language of spiritual birth, the New Testament congregation, the Spirit, God’s kingdom, and salvation.
* The closing sentence abruptly restrains further discussion, leaving the issue open.

STEP 10: ORAL CADENCE AWARENESS

* Long sentences will be retained where Harbo allows his reasoning to accumulate.
* Repetition of “view,” “congregational work,” “Lutheran Free Church,” and “larger church bodies” will not be stylistically reduced.
* The anaphora “appear … appear … appear” will be preserved.
* Causal markers such as “for,” “therefore,” “but,” and “of course” will remain explicit.
* Semicolon-like logical pivots will be preserved where natural in English rather than converted into terse contemporary prose.

STEP 11: ACADEMIC TRANSLATION

The title’s “O. L. Anderson” is retained as printed, although the body consistently has “L. O. Anderson.”

Pastor O. L. Anderson and the Guiding Principles of the Lutheran Free Church

“Folkebladet,” July 17, 1918

Elias P. Harbo

Pastor L. O. Anderson has, in Folkebladet for June 26 of this year, given an account of why he left the Lutheran Free Church.

In this account he says, among other things: “In short: because I have come to realize that the manner in which I conduct congregational work is not in harmony with the view of congregational work maintained by the Guiding Principles of the Lutheran Free Church, but is rather in harmony with the view of congregational work maintained by the larger church bodies.”

The first thing we notice in the remarkable statement quoted above is that, in Pastor Anderson’s opinion, there exist two entirely different views of congregational work in “the larger church bodies” and in the Lutheran Free Church, in that “the larger church bodies” maintain one view and the Lutheran Free Church maintains another. And so entirely different are these two views of congregational work that Pastor Anderson could not remain in the Lutheran Free Church and at the same time conduct his congregational work according to the view of congregational work maintained in “the larger church bodies.” We are very grateful to Pastor Anderson for this information, namely, that he has made the discovery, or come to the recognition, that there exist two entirely different views of congregational work in “the larger church bodies” and in the Lutheran Free Church. And of course there is an entirely different practice as well, for it is surely right to conclude that each one practices according to his view of how congregational work ought to be carried on.

Pastor Anderson therefore does not agree with those who say that there is no difference between the Lutheran Free Church and the church bodies.

Pastor Anderson declares without any reservation that “the larger church bodies” and the Lutheran Free Church maintain two entirely different views of congregational work.

And it is this view of how work ought to be carried on for and in the congregation, in order that the work may bear fruit for the life and flourishing of the congregation, which is of such exceedingly great importance to all of us who are concerned for God’s congregation, its growth and flourishing.

But the view a man has of the work for and in the congregation depends, of course, upon the view he has of the congregation itself, what the congregation is according to its essential nature, and what its calling and task are.

Pastor Anderson gives no explanation of wherein this difference between the two views consists. He gives only the declaration that the Guiding Principles of the Lutheran Free Church maintain one view and “the larger church bodies” maintain another.

Nor does he mention the points in the Guiding Principles in which the Lutheran Free Church’s view is maintained.

We will therefore, through Folkebladet, address a friendly request to Pastor Anderson that he give a more detailed explanation of wherein these two different views consist, and also name the points in the Guiding Principles which, in his understanding, maintain the Lutheran Free Church’s view of the work for and in the congregation.

This question, of how we are to work for and in the congregation in such a way that it may appear among us more as a living and free congregation, appear in its New Testament form, appear Spirit-filled and vigorous in action, is a question which all of us who are born of God regard with warm interest, and to which we are therefore undoubtedly willing to contribute our share, so that all possible light may be shed upon it, in order that we may attain the greatest possible clarity in this matter, which is of such exceedingly great importance for the kingdom of God and the salvation of souls.

But this time we shall say no more about this question.

The translation retains Harbo’s deliberate repetition, ecclesiological terminology, cumulative argument, and the marked rise in spiritual intensity in the final paragraph.
